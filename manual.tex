\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ====================================================================

The \pkg{} Python package computes slip-line fields and bearing
capacities for strip foundations and circular disks using the
\emph{method of characteristics} (MOC).
Five solver classes cover a range of material models and loading
conditions:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Class} & \textbf{Material} & \textbf{Geometry} \\
\midrule
\texttt{PrandtlSolver} & Tresca ($c_u$, weightless) & Strip footing \\
\texttt{MohrCoulombPrandtlSolver} & Mohr-Coulomb ($c$, $\phi$, weightless) & Strip footing \\
\texttt{MohrCoulombGravityPrandtlSolver} & Mohr-Coulomb + gravity ($\gamma$) & Strip footing \\
\texttt{DiskPrandtlSolver} & Tresca ($c_u$, weightless) & Disk/pipe \\
\texttt{MohrCoulombDiskPrandtlSolver} & Mohr-Coulomb ($c$, $\phi$, weightless) & Disk/punch \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

All solvers live in \texttt{slip\_line/solver.py} and are accessible
after importing the package:
\begin{lstlisting}
from slip_line.solver import (
    PrandtlSolver,
    MohrCoulombPrandtlSolver,
    MohrCoulombGravityPrandtlSolver,
    DiskPrandtlSolver,
    MohrCoulombDiskPrandtlSolver,
)
\end{lstlisting}

Three further modules build on the Tresca nets:
\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{velocity.py}] Geiringer velocity fields on the strip
        and disk nets, with checks of the plastic work and the
        virtual-work balance (Sect.~\ref{sec:velocity}).
  \item[\texttt{penetration.py}] A pipe sinking into the soil under its
        own weight, with the heave beside it tracked step by step
        (\texttt{PipePenetration}, Sect.~\ref{sec:penetration}).
  \item[\texttt{lateral.py}] A lateral push of the embedded pipe while
        it is free to move vertically, as an upper-bound mechanism,
        and a push back (\texttt{PipeLateral}, Sect.~\ref{sec:lateral}).
\end{description}

\subsection{Sign conventions and coordinate system}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The $x$-axis points \emph{to the right}; the $y$-axis points
        \emph{downward} (depth increases with $y$).
  \item Stresses are \emph{compression positive} (geotechnical
        convention).
  \item The strip foundation occupies $x \in [0,B]$, $y = 0$, with
        symmetry at $x=0$.  Only the right half is computed; the full
        field is obtained by mirror reflection.
  \item $\psi$ is the angle of the \emph{major principal stress}
        $\sigma_1$ measured counter-clockwise (CCW) from the
        $+x$-axis.  With $y$ pointing down, CCW is \emph{visually}
        clockwise in the printed figures.
  \item The \emph{modified mean stress} used internally for
        Mohr-Coulomb is
        $\pbar = p + c\cot\phi$,
        where $p = (\sigma_1+\sigma_3)/2$ is the mean stress.
  \item On a disk (pipe) the contact point is given by the angle
        $\varphi$ from the downward axis at the centre, positive
        toward $+x$; $\varphi = 0$ is the invert.  The tangent
        $\hat t = (\cos\varphi, -\sin\varphi)$ points from the invert
        toward the edge, the normal
        $\hat n = (\sin\varphi, \cos\varphi)$ from the disk into the
        soil.
  \item Contact stresses are reported as stresses \emph{from the pipe
        on the soil}: $\sigma_n$ as compression into the soil, and the
        signed shear $\tau$ on the soil along $\hat t$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Theory summary}
\label{sec:theory}
% ====================================================================

\subsection{Characteristic directions (Mohr-Coulomb)}

At a point with stress invariants $(\pbar, \psi)$, the two families of
slip lines have slopes (in the $y$-down frame):

\begin{align}
  \text{$\alpha$-line:} \quad
    \frac{dy}{dx} &= \tan\!\left(\psi - \tfrac{\pi}{4} + \tfrac{\phi}{2}\right), \\
  \text{$\beta$-line:} \quad
    \frac{dy}{dx} &= \tan\!\left(\psi + \tfrac{\pi}{4} - \tfrac{\phi}{2}\right).
\end{align}

\subsection{Kötter equations}

Without gravity ($\gamma = 0$):
\begin{align}
  \text{Along $\alpha$:} \quad \ln\pbar - 2\tanphi\,\psi &= \mathrm{const}, \\
  \text{Along $\beta$:} \quad \ln\pbar + 2\tanphi\,\psi &= \mathrm{const}.
\end{align}

With gravity (body force $+\gamma\hat{j}$, $y$ downward):
\begin{align}
  \text{Along $\alpha$:} \quad
    d\ln\pbar - 2\tanphi\,d\psi
    &= \frac{\gamma}{\pbar}\bigl[dy - \tanphi\,dx\bigr], \\
  \text{Along $\beta$:} \quad
    d\ln\pbar + 2\tanphi\,d\psi
    &= \frac{\gamma}{\pbar}\bigl[dy + \tanphi\,dx\bigr].
\end{align}

These are integrated by a predictor–corrector Riemann finite-difference
step implemented in \texttt{riemann\_step\_mc\_gravity} in
\texttt{slip\_line/slip\_line\_field.py}.

\subsection{Tresca (undrained) soil}

For $\phi = 0$ the slip lines lie at $\pm\pi/4$ to $\sigma_1$,
\begin{equation}
  \text{$\alpha$-line:}\quad \frac{dy}{dx} = \tan\!\left(\psi - \tfrac{\pi}{4}\right),
  \qquad
  \text{$\beta$-line:}\quad \frac{dy}{dx} = \tan\!\left(\psi + \tfrac{\pi}{4}\right),
\end{equation}
and the Hencky relations for the mean stress $\sigma$ (weightless) are
\begin{align}
  \text{Along $\alpha$:} \quad \sigma - 2c_u\psi &= \mathrm{const}, \\
  \text{Along $\beta$:} \quad \sigma + 2c_u\psi &= \mathrm{const}.
\end{align}
The Tresca solvers use \texttt{riemann\_step}; the velocity fields
(Sect.~\ref{sec:velocity}) and the pipe simulations
(Sects.~\ref{sec:penetration}–\ref{sec:lateral}) are Tresca only.

\subsection{Prandtl–Hill three-region mechanism}

The classical three-region mechanism (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}) consists
of:

\begin{description}
  \item[Region I (active wedge):] Under the foundation.
        $\psi = \pi/2$ for a smooth footing.
  \item[Region II (fan / centred-fan):] Log-spiral fan emanating from
        the footing corner $(B,0)$.  $\psi$ rotates from $0$ (on the
        Region~III side) to $\pi/2$ (on the Region~I side).
  \item[Region III (passive wedge):] Adjacent to the free surface.
        $\psi = 0$.
\end{description}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{mc_gravity_phi30.png}
\caption{Slip-line field for $\phi=30^\circ$, $c=1$, $B=1$,
  $\gamma B/c = 0$ (top-left panel).  Blue lines are $\alpha$-lines,
  red lines are $\beta$-lines.  The bold black segment is the foundation.}
\label{fig:mechanism}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Analytical bearing-capacity factors}

For a \emph{smooth, weightless} strip footing on Mohr-Coulomb soil the
collapse pressure is $q = c\,\Nc$ with:
\begin{align}
  \Nq &= \tan^2\!\!\left(\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{\phi}{2}\right)
          e^{\pi\tanphi}, \\
  \Nc &= (\Nq - 1)\cot\phi.
\end{align}
For $\phi=30^\circ$: $\Nq = 18.40$, $\Nc = 30.14$.

With gravity the full Terzaghi-type formula is:
\begin{equation}
  q = c\,\Nc + \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma B\,\Ngamma,
\end{equation}
where $\Ngamma$ must be obtained from the numerical MOC solver (Vesic:
$\Ngamma(30^\circ)=22.4$).

\section{Installation and requirements}
\label{sec:install}
% ====================================================================

The project is managed with
\href{https://docs.astral.sh/uv/}{uv}: Python~3.13, with NumPy and
Matplotlib declared in \texttt{pyproject.toml}.  Run scripts from the
project root; uv creates the environment and installs the dependencies
on the first run:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash,numbers=none]
uv run main.py            # interactive: strip footing or disk (Tresca)
uv run run_penetration.py # pipe sinking under its own weight
uv run run_lateral.py     # pipe sinking, pushed sideways and back
\end{lstlisting}

Scripts in the project root import the package directly
(\texttt{from slip\_line.solver import ...}).  The \texttt{test\_*.py}
and \texttt{plot\_*.py} files there are standalone scripts that print
tables or save figures in \texttt{plots/}; they are not a pytest
suite.

\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Parameter & Type & Description \\
\midrule
\texttt{cu} & float & Undrained shear strength \\
\texttt{r} & float $\in[0,1]$ & Roughness ratio ($\tau/c_u$); $0$ = smooth \\
\texttt{B} & float & Foundation \emph{half}-width \\
\texttt{n\_fan} & int & Fan discretisation steps \\
\texttt{n\_radial} & int & Radial mesh steps (default = \texttt{n\_fan}) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsubsection*{Key methods}

\begin{lstlisting}
q = solver.solve()        # collapse load (float)
lines_a = solver.all_alpha_lines()   # list of alpha-line node lists
lines_b = solver.all_beta_lines()    # list of beta-line node lists
\end{lstlisting}

The analytical result for a smooth footing ($r=0$) is $q = c_u(2+\pi)$.

\textbf{Limitation for $r > 0$.}  Region~I is a uniform-stress zone
with $\psi_A = \pi/2 - \tfrac12\arcsin r$, which is only consistent for
a smooth base.  The rough strip footing also has $q = c_u(2+\pi)$
(Hill mechanism, with the base itself a slip line), but the solver
returns a lower value (for $r = 1$: $q = c_u(1+\pi/2)$).  Use
\texttt{PrandtlSolver} with $r = 0$; roughness is handled by the disk
solvers.

\begin{center}

\subsubsection*{Convergence / mesh refinement}

Increase \texttt{n\_fan} and \texttt{n\_radial} (keeping them equal) for
higher accuracy.  Typical values:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Coarse check: \texttt{n\_fan = n\_radial = 12}
  \item Production: \texttt{n\_fan = n\_radial = 24}–\texttt{40}
\end{itemize}
The solver iterates the mesh scaling automatically so that Region~I fits
the footing exactly; no manual adjustment is needed.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{\texttt{DiskPrandtlSolver} — Tresca disk / pipe}
\label{sec:tresca_disk}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------

A rigid disk (a pipe in plane strain) of diameter $D = 2R$ is pressed
into weightless Tresca soil to the depth $z = pD$, $0 \le p \le 0.5$.
The soil touches the disk on $\varphi \in [-\theta_{\max}, \theta_{\max}]$
with $\theta_{\max} = \arccos(1-2p)$, and the contact half-width at the
surface is $B_s = D\sqrt{p(1-p)}$.  Only the right half is computed.
Region~I is built from the disk surface first; the centred fan at the
contact edge and the passive wedge (Region~III) are then sized to match
it.

\subsubsection*{Interface roughness and its direction}

With roughness $r = |\tau|/c_u$ the net meets the disk at the angle
$\omega = \tfrac12\arcsin r$, and on the disk
\begin{equation}
  \psi_\text{disk}(\varphi) = \frac{\pi}{2} - \varphi - \omega,
  \qquad
  \sigma_n = c_u\bigl(1 + 2\psi_\text{disk} + \sqrt{1-r^2}\bigr),
  \qquad
  |\tau| = r\,c_u .
\end{equation}
The fan opens by $\Delta\psi = \pi/2 - \theta_{\max} - \omega$.

The sign of $\omega$ sets the direction of the interface shear:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Default} ($\omega > 0$): the shear on the soil points
        toward the disk edge, as in a horizontal push.
  \item \texttt{vertical\_shear=True} ($\omega < 0$): the shear of
        vertical penetration.  The soil slides out toward the edge
        relative to the disk, the shear on the soil points toward the
        invert and the friction on the disk acts upward.
\end{itemize}
\texttt{collapse\_load\_Q()} integrates
\begin{equation}
  Q = 2R\int_0^{\theta_{\max}}\bigl(\sigma_n\cos\varphi + |\tau|\sin\varphi\bigr)\,d\varphi,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ with the friction on the disk acting upward.  That is consistent
with the stress field only for \texttt{vertical\_shear=True}, where
\texttt{collapse\_load\_Q()} equals twice the $F_v$ of
\texttt{forces\_on\_half\_disk()}.
For the vertical capacity of a rough disk, use
\texttt{vertical\_shear=True}.

\textbf{Signed roughness.}  A negative $r$ in a problem statement
(e.g.\ $r = -0.75$) means the shear direction of vertical penetration:
pass \texttt{r=abs(r)} and \texttt{vertical\_shear=True};
\texttt{DiskFoundationBoundary} accepts only $0 \le r \le 1$.

\textbf{Parallel zone.}  With the default direction and
$\theta_{\max} > \theta_0 = \pi/2 - \omega$ (deep penetration and high
roughness) the fan would be negative.  On the outer arc
$\varphi > \theta_0$ the roughness is then reduced so that
$\psi_\text{disk} = 0$ (parallel $\beta$-lines, no fan);
\texttt{parallel\_beta\_zone} in the result tells when this is used.

\subsubsection*{Constructor}

\begin{lstlisting}
from slip_line.boundary import DiskFoundationBoundary
from slip_line.solver   import DiskPrandtlSolver

disk = DiskFoundationBoundary(cu=1.0, D=1.0, p=0.25, r=0.5)
solver = DiskPrandtlSolver(disk, n_fan=18, n_disk=30,
                           vertical_shear=True)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Parameter & Type & Description \\
\midrule
\texttt{disk} & \texttt{DiskFoundationBoundary} & $c_u$, $D$, $p = z/D$, $r$ \\
\texttt{n\_fan}  & int & Fan steps \\
\texttt{n\_disk} & int & Steps along the contact arc \\
\texttt{n\_radial} & int & Steps in Region~III (default \texttt{n\_fan}) \\
\texttt{vertical\_shear} & bool & Shear direction of vertical penetration \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsubsection*{Key methods}

% ====================================================================
\section{Velocity fields}
\label{sec:velocity}
% ====================================================================

\texttt{slip\_line/velocity.py} builds the velocity field that belongs
to a Tresca net (associated flow) and checks it against the stress
field through the plastic work and the virtual-work balance.

\subsection{Geiringer equations}

The velocity is resolved along the slip lines,
$\mathbf V = u\,\mathbf e_\alpha + v\,\mathbf e_\beta$, with
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf e_\alpha = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta), \qquad
  \mathbf e_\beta = (-\sin\theta, \cos\theta), \qquad
  \theta = \psi - \pi/4 .
\end{equation}
Zero extension rate along both families gives
\begin{align}
  \text{Along $\alpha$:} \quad du - v\,d\psi &= 0, \\
  \text{Along $\beta$:} \quad dv + u\,d\psi &= 0 .
\end{align}
Velocity discontinuities can only occur across slip lines; they are
tangential and have constant magnitude along the line.  The equations
are integrated node by node with a trapezoidal step
(\texttt{geiringer\_step}) in the same order as the stress net.

\subsection{Boundary conditions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Outer $\alpha$-line} (Region~I from the base or invert
        $\to$ fan $\to$ Region~III): it borders soil at rest, so
        $v = 0$ on the plastic side and $u = U$, the velocity jump,
        along the whole line.
  \item \textbf{Foundation or disk}: the normal velocity equals that of
        the foundation, $\mathbf V\cdot\hat n = \mathbf V_0\cdot\hat n$;
        the soil may slip along it.  At the symmetry point this fixes
        $U$ (strip: $U = v_0/\sin(\psi_A - \pi/4)$, i.e.\ $\sqrt2\,v_0$
        for a smooth base).
  \item \textbf{Free surface}: no kinematic condition.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Plastic work, interface slip and energy balance}

In each triangle of the net the velocity is linear, the strain rate is
pure shear $\dot\gamma_{\alpha\beta}$, and the plastic work rate per
unit area is $d = -c_u\dot\gamma_{\alpha\beta}$
(\texttt{plastic\_work\_rate}).  $d < 0$ means the velocity field does
negative work against the stress field there: the two do not form a
complete solution.

Along a rough disk the slip is
$\text{slip} = (\mathbf V_0 - \mathbf V)\cdot\hat t$ (disk relative to
the soil) and the interface work rate is $w = \tau\cdot\text{slip}$
with $\tau$ the shear of the disk on the soil
(\texttt{interface\_slip()}).

\texttt{energy\_balance()} evaluates the work equation
\begin{equation}
  F\,v_0 = n_h\bigl(P + c_u U L + P_\text{interface}\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $L$ the length of the outer $\alpha$-line and $n_h = 2$ halves for a
vertical push, $1$ for a horizontal push:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Key & Meaning \\
\midrule
\texttt{F\_stress} & $F$ from the solver's contact stresses \\
\texttt{F\_virtual\_work} & $P = \sum d\,A$, $P_\text{interface} = \int w\,ds$ (signed); \\
 & equals \texttt{F\_stress} when the velocity field is correct \\
\texttt{F\_upper\_bound} & $P = \sum |d|\,A$, $P_\text{interface} = \int r c_u|\text{slip}|\,ds$ \\
\texttt{negative\_area\_fraction} & Share of the plastic area with $d < 0$ \\
\texttt{negative\_interface\_fraction} & Share of the contact arc with $w < 0$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For the vertical disk $d < 0$ in a large part of the plastic area
(39\,\% at $p = 0.25$, Fig.~\ref{fig:velocity_disk}) even for $r = 0$,
so the field is not strictly kinematically admissible.
\texttt{F\_virtual\_work} lies slightly below \texttt{F\_stress}
(3.631 against 3.639 at $p = 0.25$, \texttt{n\_fan=18, n\_disk=30}) and
converges to it with a finer mesh; \texttt{F\_upper\_bound} is 4.010.

\section{Pipe penetration under self-weight}
\label{sec:penetration}
% ====================================================================

\texttt{PipePenetration} (\texttt{slip\_line/penetration.py}) lets a
pipe of diameter $D$, roughness $r$ and weight $W$ (per unit length)
sink quasi-statically into weightless Tresca soil, and tracks the heave
of the surface beside it.  The interface shear opposes the slip: the
soil slides out along the pipe, so the friction on the pipe acts upward
(\texttt{DiskPrandtlSolver} with \texttt{vertical\_shear=True}).

\subsection{Algorithm}

The pipe is pushed down in small steps $\Delta w$.  In each step:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Contact.}  The pipe meets the current soil surface at
        $x_c$.  The slip-line field is that of \texttt{DiskPrandtlSolver}
        with a \emph{flat} surface at the contact level, i.e.\ at the
        effective embedment $d$ = invert depth below that level.  Heave
        raises the contact level, so $d > w$.
  \item \textbf{Heave.}  The Geiringer field for a unit downward pipe
        velocity gives the upward velocity $v_\text{up}(x)$ of the free
        surface (Region~III).  The surface is raised by
        $v_\text{up}\,\Delta w$; the horizontal component is not used,
        since the field assumes a flat surface.
  \item \textbf{Volume.}  The soil is incompressible: the soil pushed
        down under the pipe equals the heave beside it.  The Geiringer
        field satisfies this to first order; the heave increment is
        scaled to remove the discretisation error.
  \item \textbf{Stop.}  The pipe sinks while the collapse load $V(d)$ is
        below $W$; the last step is shortened so that $V = W$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{API}

\begin{lstlisting}
from slip_line.penetration import PipePenetration

sim = PipePenetration(D=1.0, cu=1.0, r=0.8, W=2.8)
states = sim.run()                   # list of PenetrationState
s1 = states[-1]                      # equilibrium, V = W
print(s1.w, s1.d, s1.d - s1.w)       # settlement, embedment, heave
\end{lstlisting}

Constructor arguments: \texttt{D}, \texttt{cu}, \texttt{r}, \texttt{W},
the step \texttt{dw} (default $2.5\times10^{-5}$), the surface grid
spacing \texttt{dx} ($2.5\times10^{-4}$), its extent \texttt{x\_max}
(default $1.5D$), and \texttt{n\_fan}, \texttt{n\_disk}.
A \texttt{PenetrationState} has the settlement \texttt{w}, the effective
embedment \texttt{d}, the contact half-width \texttt{x\_c}, the end of
the plastic zone \texttt{x\_end}, the load \texttt{V}, the heave
\texttt{heave} on \texttt{sim.x} and the volume correction
\texttt{scale}.  \texttt{sim.load(d)} is the collapse load at
embedment $d$, and \texttt{sim.areas(w, heave)} the volume balance.

\texttt{run\_lateral.py}):

\section{Lateral push of a pipe}
\label{sec:lateral}
% ====================================================================

\texttt{PipeLateral} (\texttt{slip\_line/lateral.py}) pushes the
embedded pipe sideways toward $+x$ in small steps $\Delta u$ while it
is free to move vertically: the vertical load $V = W$ stays constant.
The start is the end of a \texttt{PipePenetration} run.  The soil
surface (berm in front, trench behind) is tracked step by step.  The
analysis is kinematic (upper bound).

\subsection{Mechanism}

The pipe moves with unit speed along $(\cos\beta, \sin\beta)$, so
$\beta > 0$ is downward.  Its trailing side separates from the soil
beyond $\varphi = -\beta$, and the soil touches the pipe on
$\varphi \in [\varphi_\text{start}, \theta_R]$, $\theta_R$ being the
right contact angle, with
\begin{equation}
  \varphi_\text{start} =
  \begin{cases}
    -\min(\beta, \varphi_L) & \beta \ge 0, \\
    -\beta & \beta < 0 \text{ (the pipe rises and leaves the invert).}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
$\varphi_L$ is the largest trailing contact angle.  Against the
original surface it is the current left contact angle (diving steeper
than that cuts into the soil, with a velocity jump along the outer
$\alpha$-line).  Against the trench wall the pipe has cut itself there
is no limit.

The net (\texttt{OneSidedDiskSolver}) is that of
\texttt{DiskPrandtlSolver} on this contact chain, but one-sided: all
soil flows toward $+x$ through a fan and passive wedge at the right
edge.  On the contact $\psi = \pi/2 - \varphi - \omega$.  The free
surface in front is a straight line from the right contact point,
falling away from the pipe at the angle $\alpha$ of a least-squares fit
to the berm over the passive wedge's extent; there $\psi = \alpha$ and
$\sigma = c_u\bigl(1 + 2(\psi - \alpha)\bigr)$.

\subsection{Work equation}

The Geiringer field on the net gives the dissipation per unit pipe
speed,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{D} = \sum |d|\,A + c_u U L + r c_u \int |\text{slip}|\,ds,
\end{equation}
(plastic shear, the jump along the outer $\alpha$-line, interface
slip).  With $V = W$ constant the work equation gives the horizontal
force on the full pipe per unit length,
\begin{equation}
  H\cos\beta + W\sin\beta = \mathcal{D}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  H = \frac{\mathcal{D} - W\sin\beta}{\cos\beta}.
\end{equation}
The pipe moves in the direction $\beta$ (with the net angle $\omega$)
that minimises $H$, the normality rule of the upper-bound yield
envelope.  The first step scans $\beta$ from $-20^\circ$ to $85^\circ$;
later steps search locally around the previous mechanism
(golden-section search).  If the search ends on an invalid
(incomplete) net, the best valid mechanism it has seen is used.

\subsection{Net angle: free or reversing friction}

\textbf{Free net angle} (default).  $\omega$ is chosen with $\beta$ to
minimise $H$.  The net then need not match $r$: it is nearly smooth
($|\omega| \lesssim 10^\circ$), and $r$ enters only through the slip
work.

\textbf{Reversing friction} (\texttt{reversing=True}).  The net carries
the interface friction, $\tau = c_u\sin2\omega = \pm r c_u$.  The slip
changes sign along the pipe: near the invert the pipe slides toward
the edge relative to the soil (slip $> 0$), near the edge the soil
slides up past the pipe (slip $< 0$).  A uniform $\omega$ cannot
match both, so the friction reverses at $\varphi_s$:
\begin{equation}
  \omega(\varphi) = \omega_r\,
  \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{\varphi_s - \varphi}{\omega_r}, -1, 1\right),
  \qquad \omega_r = \tfrac12\arcsin r .
\end{equation}
In the transition $\varphi_s \pm \omega_r$ the angle
$\psi = \pi/2 - \varphi_s$ is constant and the friction is only partly
mobilised: the soil sticks to the pipe there.  $\varphi_s$ is placed
where the slip of the mechanism's own velocity field is zero, found by
bisection for each $\beta$ (if the slip keeps one sign, the friction
does not reverse); only $\beta$ is then searched.  The result is
self-consistent: the friction nowhere acts against the slip, and $H$
is lower than with the free net.

In both modes $\omega$ is reduced on the outer arc where the fan would
be negative ($\pi/2 - \varphi - \omega < \alpha$), so that
$\psi = \alpha$ there (parallel zone, as in \texttt{DiskPrandtlSolver}).

\subsection{Surface update}

In each step the pipe moves $\Delta u$ sideways and
$\Delta u\tan\beta$ down (path length $\Delta s = \Delta u/\cos\beta$).
The free surface in front rises by the Geiringer velocity through the
sloping surface times $\Delta s$
($v_\text{up} + v_x\tan\alpha$ per unit width), and the soil the pipe
moves into is removed; the rise is scaled so that the soil volume is
conserved.  $\alpha$ is then refitted to the new surface.  The soil on
the left (trench wall or original surface) is rigid, and the trench
behind the pipe stays open.

\subsection{Push back}

\texttt{reversed(state)} sets up a push back toward $-x$ from a state.
It is computed in the mirror image $x \to -x$, where it is again a push
toward $+x$: the trench is then in front (its rising wall gives
$\alpha < 0$) and the berm behind.  Map $u$, $x$ and $H$ back with a
change of sign.  At the turn the push starts with $H > 0$ toward $+x$,
i.e.\ a holding force.

\subsection{Consistency with step 1 and step length}

With reversing friction and a flat surface the one-sided mechanism
carries $V = 2.69$\,kN/m at $H = 0$ (\texttt{n\_disk=30}; 2.70 with
\texttt{n\_disk=90}), where the stress solution of the penetration step
gives 2.80.  The stress solution thus overestimates the rough pipe's
vertical capacity by a few per cent.  $H$ is therefore negative at the
start of the push ($-0.29$\,kN/m with reversing friction,
$-0.14$\,kN/m with the free net), and the direction changes fast
during the first millimetres.
\texttt{run(..., du=...)} is therefore called with 0.25\,mm steps for
the first 50\,mm (and the first 10\,mm of the push back), and 1\,mm
steps after that.  With halved steps the results change by about 2\,\%
with reversing friction ($\beta$ jumps by $\sim1^\circ$ when
$\varphi_s$ moves between mesh nodes) and by less than 0.1\,\% with the
free net.

\subsection{API}

\begin{lstlisting}

s = states[-1]

\end{lstlisting}

reversing=False)}; \texttt{after\_penetration(sim, state, x\_min=-1.5,
x\_max=2.5, **kw)} builds the model and start state (the right-half
heave mirrored to the left) on a surface grid from \texttt{x\_min} to
\texttt{x\_max}.

A \texttt{LateralState} has the displacement \texttt{u}, the invert
depth \texttt{w}, the surface \texttt{eta} (positive upward) on

\subsection{Example 4: Effect of gravity — $N_\gamma$ extraction}
\label{ex:gravity}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------

Use \texttt{MohrCoulombGravityPrandtlSolver} to study the
dimensionless group $\gamma B/c$ and extract the implied $\Ngamma$.

From the formula $Q/(cB) = \Nc + \frac{1}{2}(\gamma B/c)\Ngamma$:
\begin{equation}
  \Ngamma = \frac{2\,[Q/(cB) - \Nc]}{\gamma B / c}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lstlisting}
import numpy as np
from slip_line.solver import MohrCoulombGravityPrandtlSolver

c   = 1.0
B   = 1.0
phi = np.radians(30.0)
Nc  = 30.14    # analytical for phi=30

print(f"{'gB/c':>5}  {'Q/cB':>8}  {'N_gamma':>8}")
for gBc in [1.0, 2.5, 5.0, 10.0]:
    gamma = gBc   # since c = B = 1
    s = MohrCoulombGravityPrandtlSolver(
        c=c, phi=phi, gamma=gamma, B=B,
        n_fan=24, n_radial=24,
    )
    res = s.solve()
    QcB = res['q_over_cB']
    Ngam = 2.0 * (QcB - Nc) / gBc
    print(f"{gBc:>5.1f}  {QcB:>8.4f}  {Ngam:>8.3f}")
\end{lstlisting}

Plot $\sigma_{yy}(x)$ along the foundation for several $\gamma B/c$
values.

\begin{lstlisting}
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from slip_line.solver import MohrCoulombGravityPrandtlSolver

c, phi, B = 1.0, np.radians(30.0), 1.0

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 4))
for gBc in [0, 1, 2.5, 5]:
    s = MohrCoulombGravityPrandtlSolver(
        c=c, phi=phi, gamma=float(gBc), B=B,
        n_fan=24, n_radial=24,
    )
    s.solve()
    x, sig = s.contact_pressure_curve()
    ax.plot(x / B, sig / c, lw=1.8, label=rf'$\gamma B/c={gBc}$')

ax.set_xlabel(r'$x\,/\,B$')
ax.set_ylabel(r'$\sigma_{yy}\,/\,c$')
ax.set_title(r'Contact pressure, smooth strip footing ($\phi=30^\circ$)')
ax.legend()
ax.grid(alpha=0.3)
plt.tight_layout()
plt.savefig('contact_pressure.png', dpi=140)
\end{lstlisting}

The curve for $\gamma=0$ is uniform (as expected for the weightless
solution); for $\gamma>0$ the pressure peaks at $x=0$ (symmetry axis)
and equals $q_\text{corner} = c\Nc$ at $x=B$ regardless of $\gamma$,
because the corner is at the free surface.

\subsection{Example 8: $N_c$ convergence study}
\label{ex:convergence}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------

Check how $N_c$ depends on the mesh.

\begin{lstlisting}
import numpy as np
from slip_line.solver import MohrCoulombPrandtlSolver

phi    = np.radians(30.0)
Nc_ref = 30.1396

print(f"{'n_fan':>6}  {'Nc':>10}  {'error':>10}")
for n in [4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48]:
    s  = MohrCoulombPrandtlSolver(c=1.0, phi=phi, B=1.0, n_fan=n)
    Nc = s.solve()
    print(f"{n:>6}  {Nc:>10.6f}  {abs(Nc-Nc_ref):>10.2e}")
\end{lstlisting}

Every mesh gives $N_c = 30.139628$: the weightless field is exact for
any \texttt{n\_fan}, and the error of $2.8\times10^{-5}$ is the rounding
of \texttt{Nc\_ref}.  Mesh refinement matters for the gravity solver
(Sect.~\ref{ex:gravity}) and the disk solvers.

\section{Troubleshooting}
\label{sec:trouble}
% ====================================================================

\begin{description}
  \item[Negative bearing capacity or NaN:]
        Check that \texttt{phi} is in \emph{radians} and that
        $\gamma \geq 0$.  Very coarse meshes (\texttt{n\_fan} $< 6$)
        can produce degenerate intersections; increase resolution.

  \item[Region~I does not reach $x=0$:]
        The iterative foundation-fitting (Sect.~\ref{sec:mcgrav}) runs
        up to 12 Picard steps.  If it still fails, the geometry is
        extreme ($\gamma B/c \gg 1$ combined with large $\phi$); try a
        finer mesh.

  \item[$N_\gamma$ values larger than textbook:]
        The terminal step of Region~I uses a straight-line $\alpha$-shot
        from the last interior node to the foundation.  This
        over-estimates the path integral for large $\gamma$, leading to
        a slightly inflated $N_\gamma$ for small $\gamma B/c$.  Use
        $\gamma B/c \approx 2$–$5$ for the most accurate extraction.

  \item[Disk solver \texttt{KeyError}:]
        The vertical load is \texttt{result['Q\_per\_unit\_length']}.
        Print \texttt{result.keys()} to inspect all available
        quantities.

  \item[Roughness ratio r must be in {[0, 1]}:]
        A negative $r$ means the shear direction of vertical
        penetration: use \texttt{r=abs(r)} with
        \texttt{vertical\_shear=True} (Sect.~\ref{sec:tresca_disk}).

  \item[\texttt{Plastic zone reaches x\_max}:]
        The surface grid of \texttt{PipePenetration} or
        \texttt{PipeLateral} is too short for the mechanism; increase
        \texttt{x\_max} (or \texttt{x\_max} in
        \texttt{after\_penetration}).

  \item[\texttt{No valid mechanism} in \texttt{PipeLateral}:]
        No direction $\beta$ in the scan gave a complete net at this
        geometry, so the one-sided net cannot model it.

  \item[$\beta$ jumps from step to step with reversing friction:]
        The switch $\varphi_s$ moves between nodes of the contact
        chain, and $\beta$ follows by about $1^\circ$; the results
        change by about 2\,\% with halved steps
        (Sect.~\ref{sec:lateral}).
\end{description}
